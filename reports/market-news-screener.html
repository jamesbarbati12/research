Market News Screener.

A real-time screener that tracks anything that could move a stock — not just confirmed news, but the rumors and pre-event buildup that often move price more than the eventual confirmation, because by the time something is confirmed, the surprise is already priced in. Every category's assumed severity is then backtested against real historical price data and re-ranked when the data disagrees with the assumption.

The thesis

WHY RUMORS MATTER

Most news screeners only track confirmed events — earnings releases, completed M&A deals, formal SEC filings. But by the time a deal is confirmed, the market has usually already reacted to the rumor that preceded it. The confirmation isn't the surprise; the rumor was. This screener treats unconfirmed reports, pre-event media buildup, social sentiment spikes, and rumor denials as first-class signal categories — not noise to filter out.

The interesting part isn't the hypothesis. It's what happened when the hypothesis got tested against real price data.

Pipeline

ARCHITECTURE
1
Ingest
Polls four independent sources every 60-90s: SEC EDGAR's real-time 8-K/13D/13G filing feed (no key required, covers every public filer), Finnhub general + per-ticker news, Yahoo Finance as a free supplementary source, and FMP when a paid key is available. A rotating batch cycles through a ~450-ticker watchlist so per-ticker sources stay under free-tier rate limits.
2
Classify
A regex/keyword first pass sorts each headline into one of 26 categories — including rumor_speculation, media_anticipation, social_sentiment_spike, and rumor_denial alongside standard categories like earnings, guidance, and M&A. Low-confidence matches can fall back to an LLM classifier for anything the rules don't confidently catch.
3
Score
Each category carries a base severity (Tier 1-4) and an expected average price move, scaled by the ticker's market-cap bucket — a microcap moves more than a mega-cap on the same headline, so the same category produces a different estimated move depending on company size.
4
Backtest & recalibrate
Every category's tier and expected move started as an honest, labeled placeholder. A backtest script pulls real daily price history for every ingested ticker, measures the actual close-to-close reaction around each headline, and — once a category has enough real examples — replaces the placeholder with a measured value. If the real data disagrees with the original severity assumption, the tier gets reassigned.

What the data actually showed

REAL BACKTEST RESULTS

After a production run against thousands of real, live-ingested headlines, thirteen categories had enough real samples to calibrate. Several categories moved tiers — meaning the original hand-picked severity ranking was measurably wrong in places, and the system corrected itself:

The single biggest finding: sec_filing_8k_material — a routine SEC filing category originally ranked Tier 2 — measured a 9.98% average price move across 1,976 real filings, making it the single largest mover in the entire table. Meanwhile rumor_speculation, originally assumed to be the biggest mover and ranked Tier 1, measured only 1.68% across 81 real examples. The data didn't just fail to confirm the original hypothesis — it flipped it.

One category's drop is worth reading closely: ma_definitive_agreement — a confirmed, signed M&A deal — dropped from an assumed 15% move (Tier 1) to a measured 1.63% (Tier 3) across 26 real examples. Read generously, that's consistent with the project's core thesis rather than a contradiction of it: if the deal rumor already moved the stock, the formal confirmation has little left to react to.
